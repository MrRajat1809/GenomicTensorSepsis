GenomicTensorSepsis

Words used on this page

Public studies of blood RNA in sepsis, prepared to be used together

When a patient has sepsis, the white cells of the blood change what they do, and the change shows in their RNA, the working copies of genes that a cell makes when it uses them. Research groups have measured this RNA in blood samples of patients with sepsis and deposited their measurements in public repositories.

GenomicTensorSepsis gathers these studies and makes them usable together. It searched the repositories, chose the studies that measured blood RNA of patients with sepsis, reprocessed each one from the files its authors deposited, gave every row of every table a gene from one reference list, and described every sample from its records, keeping the record or sentence that each label came from. Nothing is removed: repeat deposits, repeated samples of one patient and samples of doubtful quality are marked, so that each user can choose.

This page shows how the resource was built and what it holds, with worked examples on its real samples.

        0.

        What the method does

        The resource was built in steps, each working on the output of the one before: the studies were found and screened, every study platform was turned into one table of numbers from its own files, the rows were related to genes, every sample was described from its records, repeat deposits and patients were recognized, the data were checked against facts the curation did not decide, and the differences between studies were removed in tables of their own. One chapter per step follows.

        Each chapter has four parts: the Equation, an interactive Simulation, a Worked example on named real samples, and the Result of the step for the whole resource. For a gentler introduction, see How it works.

        1.

        Finding and choosing the studies

        Every search term was run separately in every repository, a study held in several repositories was counted once, and every candidate study received one decision: included, or excluded for the first reason, in a fixed order, that applies to it. The searches were broad on purpose; the rules then decide on what the records say.

        2.

        From array files to numbers

        An Affymetrix array is read from the file its scanner wrote and processed by RMA: the background light is removed from every probe, the arrays of a study platform are given one distribution of intensities, and the probes of each probe set are combined into one log₂ value. Each study platform is processed on its own, so that adding or removing a study never changes the numbers of another.

        3.

        From RNA-seq counts to numbers

        RNA-seq counts the reads that fall on each gene. A count means something only relative to the size of its library, so it is divided by an effective library size and given on the log₂ scale as counts per million, after a small prior count is added so that a gene without reads still has a value.

        4.

        From probes to genes

        The rows of a table are whatever the technology measures: probe sets, probes or genes. Every row is related to the genes of one reference list, that of the HUGO Gene Nomenclature Committee (HGNC), and beside every table a gene-level table holds one row per gene, built only from the rows that map to exactly one gene.

        5.

        Labels from the records

        What a sample is, sepsis or a healthy control, on which day and from which patient, is written by the investigators in free text that differs from study to study. Every study platform has its own written rules that turn these wordings into labels from fixed lists, and every label is stored with the field, the wording and the rule it came from.

        6.

        Repeat deposits and patients

        The same array is often deposited again in a later study, and one patient often gives several samples. Counting either twice gives one person more weight and lets a test sample be seen in training, so the resource recognizes both and marks them, without deleting any record.

        7.

        Sex read from the expression

        Genes of the Y chromosome are expressed in the blood of men and XIST in the blood of women. Reading sex from these genes without looking at the record, and only then comparing, tests whether each column of a table carries the record it is attached to.

        8.

        The sepsis score and the AUROC

        A few genes are known to change in the blood in sepsis. A score built from them by other investigators, before this resource existed, should separate sepsis from healthy controls within every study; the area under the ROC curve (AUROC) measures how well it does, with labels and genes that the curation could not adjust.

        9.

        Removing the differences between studies

        The same blood measured in two laboratories gives numbers that differ for reasons that have nothing to do with the patient. The correction removes these differences gene by gene: on the healthy controls of each study that has enough of them, and by matched groups where it does not. The per-study tables are left as processed; the corrected values go into two new tables, for bulk blood and for PBMC.

        10.

        The held-out test

        A correction cannot be checked on the studies it corrects, where the true values are unknown. It can be checked where the answer is known: hide the healthy controls of a study that has them, correct the study from its other samples, and measure how close the hidden controls land to the healthy controls of the other studies.

        11.

        Benchmark splits and leakage

        A benchmark fixes the tasks, the samples and how they are split into training and test, so that methods are judged by the same test. Its main danger is leakage: information about the test samples reaching the training, which makes a classifier look better than it is, often by a wide margin. Four common forms of leakage are ruled out by the splits.
